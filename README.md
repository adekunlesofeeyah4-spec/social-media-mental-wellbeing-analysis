# Social Media Addiction & Mental Well-Being Analysis
Analysis of social media usage, addiction, and mental well-being patterns using Python and Power BI.
## Project Overview
This project explores the relationship between social media usage, addiction levels, and mental well-being. The analysis examines differences across age groups, gender groups, social media platforms, occupation, relationship status, and other behavioral characteristics to identify patterns associated with mental well-being.
## Dataset
The dataset contains approximately 1,500 participants.
### Key dataset characteristics include:
- Average age: 29.37 years
- Minimum age: 15 years
- Maximum age: 44 years
- Average daily social media usage: 4.52 hours
- Average mental well-being score: 6.43
The dataset contains information relating to social media usage, addiction level, mental well-being, age, gender, occupation, relationship status, FOMO, loneliness, self-esteem, and late-night usage.
## Analytical Questions
1A. Age and Mental Well-Being
How does mental well-being score vary across age groups?
1B. Characteristics of Age Groups
How do occupation and relationship status vary among the age groups with the highest and lowest well-being?
2A. Platform Usage
Which primary platform has the highest daily usage hours?
2B. Late-Night Usage
Are the platforms being used more late at night?
3A. Addiction and Mental Well-Being
How does mental well-being score relate to addiction level?
3B. Addiction Severity
Is well-being consistently decreasing as addiction severity increases?
4A. Gender and Mental Well-Being
How does mental well-being score differ across gender groups?
4B. Characteristics Associated with Lower Well-Being
What characteristics are associated with lower well-being within each gender?
## Tools Used
- Python — data exploration, analysis, aggregation, and visualization
- Power BI — interactive visualization and dashboard development
## Analysis Approach
The dataset was explored using Python to examine patterns in mental well-being, social media usage, addiction levels, age groups, and gender.
The analysis also examined occupation, relationship status, loneliness, FOMO, self-esteem, and late-night platform usage to identify characteristics associated with differences in mental well-being.
Power BI was then used to communicate the key findings through visualizations and an interactive dashboard.
## Key Findings
- The average daily social media usage was 4.52 hours, while the average mental well-being score was 6.43.
- Snapchat had the highest average daily usage at 4.95 hours, while Instagram had the lowest at 4.44 hours.
- Instagram had the highest number of late-night users, while Snapchat had the lowest.
- TikTok had slightly more users who did not use the platform at night than users who did.
- Mental well-being varied across age groups. The Early 40s recorded the highest average mental well-being score at 6.54, while the Early 30s recorded the lowest at 6.36.
- Among the Early 30s, students who were single had the highest count.
- Among the Early 40s, working professionals who were single had the highest count.
- High addiction was the most common addiction level across male, female, and non-binary participants.
- Mental well-being decreased as addiction severity increased.
- Average mental well-being scores were 6.47 for males, 6.40 for females, and 6.38 for non-binary participants. The differences between gender groups were small.
- Within each gender group, participants with high addiction had the highest proportion of lower-well-being participants among the addiction categories examined.
- The occupation with the highest proportion of lower-well-being participants varied by gender.
- Single participants had the highest proportion of lower-well-being participants across the gender groups.
- The lower-well-being group showed higher mean and median loneliness, FOMO, and self-esteem scores across the gender groups.
